Meal prep

Week of October 12

Cinnamon Roll Overnight Oats, a cold black-eyed pea and collard salad with cornbread, and a Thai red curry built on roasted tofu. Collards are at their Northeast peak right now, late September through the first frosts, which is when they are cheapest and the leaves are sweetest, and the curry leans on storage onions, ginger and a jar of paste rather than anything that has to be summer-fresh. The three separate cleanly on all of rule 6's axes: rolled oats, cornmeal and brown rice for the carb, Greek yogurt, black-eyed peas and soy for the protein, and American, Southern US and Thai for the flavour base.

Prep order
  1. The night before prep day, soak 600g of black-eyed peas in a bowl twice as large as looks necessary. This is the only overnight step and it cuts about thirty minutes off the boil.
  2. Rice cooker on first: 400g of brown rice to 850ml of water, lid down, forgotten about for forty-five minutes.
  3. Peas onto the stovetop to simmer and the oven up to 425F at the same time. While both run, press the tofu under a heavy pan and cut every vegetable for the week in one pass.
  4. Tofu cubes into the oven for twenty-five minutes, then the cornbread in after them at 400F for twenty to twenty-five. The oven does the two jobs back to back rather than side by side.
  5. Curry on the stovetop while the cornbread bakes: fry the paste out, build the sauce, fold the roasted tofu in last so it keeps its edges.
  6. Massage, dress and box the collard salad cold at the end, then fill the six oat jars, which takes four minutes. Total active time: about 1h40.

Cinnamon Roll Overnight Oats

6 servings10 min active30g protein5.5g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 1 2/3 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 scoops (90g) — from the bulk tub, not on the grocery list
  • Water, 2 cups (480ml)
  • Light brown sugar, 1/3 cup packed (70g)
  • Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp
  • Salt, a large pinch

Method

  1. Whisk the protein powder into the 480ml of water in a jug until no lumps remain, before it goes anywhere near the yogurt. Powder stirred straight into yogurt clumps and will not come apart later.
  2. Stir the brown sugar, cinnamon and salt into the Greek yogurt in a large bowl until the sugar has dissolved and the cream has gone a pale caramel colour.
  3. Add the oats and the protein water to the bowl and stir until every oat is coated and the mixture is loose enough to pour.
  4. Divide between six jars or lidded containers, about 265g each, and refrigerate uncovered for ten minutes before sealing so condensation does not collect on the lids.
  5. Leave at least six hours before the first jar is eaten. These are better on day two than day one.

30g protein and 5.5g fibre per serving. Keeps 6 days sealed in the fridge, eaten cold straight from the jar — no reheating and no day-of step at all. If a jar has tightened up by day five, loosen it with a tablespoon of water.

Black-Eyed Pea and Collard Salad with Cornbread

6 servings40 min active30g protein17g fibreSouthern US

Ingredients

  • Black-eyed peas, dried, 1 lb 5 oz (600g)
  • Collard greens, 2 bunches / about 1 1/2 lb (680g), yielding about 1 lb 2 oz (500g) trimmed leaf
  • Roma tomatoes, 3 / about 10 oz (300g)
  • Red onion, 1 medium / about 6 oz (170g)
  • Garlic, 3 cloves
  • Lemon, 1
  • Vegetable oil, 1/3 cup (80ml)
  • Ground cumin, 2 tsp
  • Smoked paprika, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 1/2 tsp
  • Salt, 2 tsp
  • Corn muffin mix, 2 boxes / 17 oz (482g)
  • Water for the cornbread, 2/3 cup (160ml)

Method

  1. Soak the 600g of black-eyed peas in plenty of cold water the night before prep day, in a bowl twice as big as looks necessary — they roughly double.
  2. Drain the peas, cover with fresh water by two inches, bring to a boil and simmer 25 to 30 minutes until tender but still holding their shape. Taste one at 25 minutes, because a mushy pea on prep day is a mushy salad by day six.
  3. Drain the peas and spread them on a tray to cool completely. Dressing them hot turns the collards grey.
  4. Heat the oven to 400F (200C), stir the two boxes of corn muffin mix together with 160ml of water until only just combined, pour into a greased 9 by 13 inch pan and bake 20 to 25 minutes until the top springs back under a finger.
  5. Strip the collard leaves off their stems, stack the leaves, roll the stack up like a cigar and slice across into ribbons about 5mm wide.
  6. Put the ribbons in a large bowl with 1 tsp of the salt and a tablespoon of the oil, and massage them with your hands for a full minute until they darken and go slack. This is the step that lets raw collards hold six days instead of going leathery, so do not skip it.
  7. Whisk the remaining oil with the juice of the lemon, the grated garlic, the cumin, smoked paprika, cayenne and the rest of the salt into a loose dressing.
  8. Fold the cooled peas, the diced tomato and the finely sliced red onion through the collards, pour the dressing over and toss hard so every ribbon is coated.
  9. Divide between six containers. Cut the cornbread into six squares and store those in a separate box or bag.

30g protein and 17g fibre per serving. Keeps 6 days and is genuinely better on day three than day one — the massaged collards firm up rather than wilt. Eaten cold, no reheating. Store the cornbread separately or it soaks up dressing and goes soggy; fifteen seconds in the microwave brings a square back if you want it warm. The cayenne will climb through the week as it sits.

Thai Red Curry with Roasted Tofu

6 servings50 min active29g protein9.5g fibreThai

Ingredients

  • Extra-firm tofu, 2 blocks / 2 lb (907g)
  • Thai red curry paste, 1 jar / 4 oz (113g), all of it
  • Coconut milk, 2 cans / 27 fl oz (800ml)
  • Water, 1 cup (250ml)
  • Red bell peppers, 2 / about 14 oz (400g)
  • Frozen sweet peas, 1 1/2 cups (200g)
  • Yellow onion, 1 large / about 8 oz (225g)
  • Jalapenos, 2, sliced with the seeds in
  • Fresh ginger, 2 tbsp grated (30g)
  • Garlic, 3 cloves
  • Soy sauce, 2 tbsp (30ml)
  • Light brown sugar, 1 tbsp (15g)
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp
  • Cilantro, 1 bunch, for serving
  • Brown rice, 2 1/4 cups dry (400g)

Method

  1. Start the brown rice in the rice cooker, 400g of rice to 850ml of water. It takes about forty-five minutes and needs no attention, so build everything else around it.
  2. Drain both blocks of tofu, wrap them in a clean towel, set a heavy pan on top and leave them to press for twenty minutes while you cut the vegetables.
  3. Cut the pressed tofu into 3/4 inch (2cm) cubes, toss with a tablespoon of the oil and a pinch of salt, and roast at 425F (220C) for twenty-five minutes until the edges are gold and firm. Roasted cubes hold their shape in sauce for six days; raw ones turn to custard by day three.
  4. Heat the remaining 2 tablespoons of oil in a large pot over medium heat and fry the whole 113g jar of curry paste for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring constantly, until it darkens and smells sharp rather than raw.
  5. Add the sliced onion, the grated ginger and the grated garlic and cook five minutes until the onion has gone translucent.
  6. Pour in both cans of coconut milk and the 250ml of water, then add the soy sauce, brown sugar, cayenne and the two sliced jalapenos with their seeds, and simmer ten minutes.
  7. Add the sliced red peppers and simmer five minutes more, then stir in the frozen peas and the roasted tofu and take the pot straight off the heat. The peppers should keep some bite or they will be mush by day five.
  8. Divide the rice between six containers and ladle the curry over it, or keep the rice in separate boxes if you would rather it stayed less saucy.

29g protein and 9.5g fibre per serving. Keeps 6 days. Reheat in the microwave for 3 minutes covered, stirring halfway so the coconut milk does not split at the edges. One day-of step: a small handful of torn cilantro over the top after reheating — stand the bunch in a glass of water in the fridge and it will last the week. The heat climbs as the jalapeno seeds and the paste sit, so day six will be noticeably hotter than day one; the Greek yogurt you eat with dinner is the brake.
