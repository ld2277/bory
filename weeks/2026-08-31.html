Meal prep

Week of August 31

Cinnamon roll overnight oats, a huevos rancheros grain bowl and a lentil bolognese — the last full week of Northeast summer produce, so the salsa roja is built from fresh Roma tomatoes and roasted poblanos rather than a can, and the bolognese soffritto is cheap while carrots and celery still are. The three share nothing: oats, brown rice and whole wheat pasta for grain; Greek yogurt with powder, black beans with eggs, and brown lentils for protein; American, Mexican and Italian for flavour. Meals give 88g protein, plus the 40g shake and about 19g of Greek yogurt with dinner, for 147g a day. Fibre lands near 38g. No expensive-aisle item was needed at all this week, which is why it clears the ceiling with $4.95 to spare.

Prep order
  1. Brown rice into the rice cooker first — 450g dry with water to its line — and forget about it for 45 minutes.
  2. Start the black beans: cover 300g of dried beans with plenty of water, bring to a hard boil for 10 minutes, then drop to a bare simmer with half an onion and two garlic cloves for about 70 minutes. This runs unattended alongside everything else.
  3. Boil the eggs while the beans come up: 12 eggs, 10 minutes at a rolling boil, then straight into cold water. Leave them in the shell — peeled eggs only hold about four days.
  4. Broil the salsa vegetables: Roma tomatoes halved, poblanos whole, onion in wedges and unpeeled garlic on a sheet pan, 12 minutes under the broiler until blackened in patches. Steam the poblanos under a bowl for 10 minutes, then slip the skins off.
  5. Blend the salsa in two batches — the small blender will not take it all at once — with the jalapenos, lime juice and salt. Taste and add the second jalapeno's seeds if you want it properly hot.
  6. Start the bolognese while the salsa vegetables roast: soffritto of onion, carrot and celery in oil for 10 minutes, then lentils, crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, oregano, red pepper flakes and water, and simmer 35 minutes with the lid ajar.
  7. Boil the whole wheat pasta last so it is the freshest thing in the fridge, drain it, toss with a spoon of oil so it does not set into a block, and portion it under the sauce rather than stirred through.
  8. Assemble the six oat jars while the bolognese simmers: oats, yogurt, half a scoop of powder, brown sugar, cinnamon and water, stirred and lidded.
  9. Total active time: about 1h50.

Cinnamon Roll Overnight Oats

6 servings10 min active29g protein5g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 3 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 scoops (90g) — half a scoop per jar, from the bulk tub
  • Light brown sugar, 6 tbsp (75g)
  • Ground cinnamon, 3 tsp
  • Water or milk, 2 cups (480ml)
  • Salt, 1/2 tsp
  • Vanilla extract, 1 tsp (optional, if you have it)

Method

  1. Set out six jars or lidded containers with a capacity of at least 12 fl oz (350ml) each.
  2. Into each jar put 50g of rolled oats, 120g of Greek yogurt, half a scoop of protein powder, 1 tablespoon of brown sugar, half a teaspoon of cinnamon and a small pinch of salt.
  3. Add 80ml of water or milk to each jar and stir hard with a narrow spoon until no dry powder is left at the bottom — protein powder clumps if you are gentle with it.
  4. Add the vanilla if you are using it, stir once more, then lid the jars and refrigerate.
  5. Leave them at least eight hours before the first one is eaten; the oats need that long to soften properly.
  6. If a jar has thickened past what you like by day four, loosen it with a splash of water and stir.

29g protein and 5g fibre per serving. Holds the full 6 days in the fridge, eaten cold straight from the jar with no reheating and no day-of step. Fibre is the weak point of this breakfast; the day still clears 38g because the lunch and dinner both lean on legumes, and the standing bagel and banana add more on top.

Huevos Rancheros Grain Bowl

6 servings45 min active29g protein16g fibreMexican

Ingredients

  • Black beans, dried, 1 1/2 cups (300g)
  • Brown rice, 2 1/4 cups (450g) dry
  • Eggs, large, 12
  • Roma tomatoes, 12 / about 2.5 lb (1.1kg)
  • Poblano peppers, 4
  • Yellow onion, 2 medium
  • Jalapenos, 2
  • Garlic, 5 cloves
  • Limes, 3
  • Cilantro, 1 bunch
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Ground cumin, 2 tsp
  • Chili powder, 2 tsp
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Cover the dried black beans with several inches of cold water and bring them to a hard boil for ten minutes, then reduce to a bare simmer.
  2. Add half an onion cut in a wedge and two whole garlic cloves to the bean pot and simmer, partly covered, for about seventy minutes until the beans are soft but still hold their shape. Salt them only in the last ten minutes, or the skins toughen.
  3. Put the brown rice in the rice cooker with water to its own line and start it; it will hold warm until you need it.
  4. Bring a second pot of water to a rolling boil, lower the twelve eggs in, and boil them for ten minutes exactly, then move them straight into cold water. Leave them in their shells.
  5. Heat the broiler. Halve the Roma tomatoes, quarter the remaining onion and a half, and lay them cut side up on a sheet pan with the whole poblanos and the three remaining garlic cloves still in their skins.
  6. Broil for about twelve minutes, until the tomato skins have blistered and the poblanos are blackened in patches, turning the poblanos once.
  7. Put the poblanos in a bowl and cover it with a plate for ten minutes; the trapped steam loosens the skins so they slip off with your fingers. Pull the stems and most of the seeds out.
  8. Squeeze the roasted garlic out of its skins and blend it with the tomatoes, roasted onion, one whole jalapeno and the cumin and chili powder in two batches — the small blender will not take it in one — until it is a coarse salsa roja.
  9. Taste the salsa and blend in the second jalapeno, seeds and all, if you want it properly hot. It will get hotter over the week in the fridge, so stop a little short of where you want to end up.
  10. Drain the beans, discard the onion wedge, and stir two ladles of the salsa through them.
  11. Cut the peeled poblanos into strips and divide the rice, beans, poblano strips and remaining salsa between six containers, keeping the salsa in a pocket on top rather than stirred through.
  12. Store the boiled eggs separately in their shells and chop the cilantro into a small lidded pot of its own.

29g protein and 16g fibre per serving. Holds 6 days. Reheat the bowl in the microwave for 2 minutes, then peel two eggs, halve them and set them on top with a scatter of cilantro and a squeeze of lime — that is the day-of step, and it matters, because peeled eggs only keep about four days and the cilantro turns to slime if it is dressed early. The heat climbs noticeably by day four as the capsaicin migrates through the salsa.

Lentil Bolognese with Whole Wheat Pasta

6 servings55 min active30g protein17g fibreItalian

Ingredients

  • Brown lentils, dried, 1 3/4 cups (360g)
  • Whole wheat pasta, 1 lb (450g)
  • Crushed tomatoes, 2 cans / 30 oz (850g) total
  • Tomato paste, 6 oz (170g)
  • Carrots, 3 medium / about 12 oz (340g)
  • Celery, 4 stalks / about 8 oz (225g)
  • Yellow onion, 1 large
  • Garlic, 4 cloves
  • Grated parmesan, 3/4 cup (90g)
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Dried oregano, 2 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 1 1/2 tsp
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Water, about 3 cups (700ml)

Method

  1. Dice the onion, carrots and celery small and evenly — about a quarter inch — because they are the body of the sauce and large pieces stay crunchy through a long simmer.
  2. Heat the oil in a wide heavy pot and cook the soffritto over medium heat for ten minutes, stirring now and then, until the onion is translucent and the carrot has begun to soften.
  3. Add the minced garlic, the oregano and the red pepper flakes and cook for one more minute, until you can smell the garlic clearly.
  4. Stir in the tomato paste and let it fry against the bottom of the pot for two minutes; this is what gives the sauce its depth and it is worth not rushing.
  5. Add the dried lentils, both cans of crushed tomatoes and about three cups of water, and bring the pot to a boil.
  6. Drop the heat and simmer with the lid ajar for about thirty-five minutes, stirring every ten, until the lentils are tender and the sauce has thickened to the point where a spoon leaves a brief trail.
  7. Season with salt and plenty of black pepper, then stir in half the parmesan and take the pot off the heat.
  8. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to the boil and cook the whole wheat pasta one minute short of the box time, so it survives reheating without going to mush.
  9. Drain the pasta, toss it with a spoonful of oil, and let it cool for a few minutes so it does not steam itself in the containers.
  10. Divide the pasta between six containers and ladle the sauce over the top rather than stirring it through — the pasta keeps its texture far better under the sauce than in it.
  11. Leave the remaining parmesan in the fridge to add at serving.

30g protein and 17g fibre per serving. Holds 6 days. Reheat covered in the microwave for 3 minutes with a splash of water added first, stirring once halfway through; the whole wheat pasta drinks liquid as it sits and will be dry otherwise. Scatter the reserved parmesan on after reheating, not before. The red pepper flakes intensify over the week, so day six will be noticeably hotter than day one.
