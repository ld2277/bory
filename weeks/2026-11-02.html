Three picks, two of them cooked here for the first time. Rajma with jeera rice is the winter pulse dish the library has been holding since August and November is exactly its month, and the lentil bolognese leans on storage carrots, celery and onions at their Northeast autumn peak. The frozen berry and chia oats are the headline: the only recipe in the library never cooked, passed over six weeks running, and they finally fit because both mains are dried pulses with nothing from the expensive aisle, which leaves the week's one allowance free for the chia. Frozen berries are also simply the right berry in November. Separation is clean on all three axes — rolled oats, brown rice and whole wheat pasta for the grain; Greek yogurt, kidney bean and brown lentil for the protein; American, Indian and Italian for the kitchen. Fibre runs to about 70g a day, which is more than double the 30g floor and the highest on record — two dried-pulse mains plus a chia breakfast is the highest-fibre shape this library can produce, and it is worth knowing before Monday. The most expensive line of the week by a distance is the chia at $10.78, a fifth of the recipe spend for 60g of use; the breakfast notes carry the one-line swap if you would rather not buy a 2 lb tub.

Prep order
  1. The night before: cover the kidney beans with cold water and leave them on the counter. That is the only overnight step in the week, and skipping it adds an hour to prep day.
  2. Beans on first — hard boil, then a 45 to 55 minute simmer. They are the long pole and everything else is built around them. Put the rice cooker on with the jeera rice at the same time and forget it.
  3. While the beans simmer, build the bolognese: soffritto for 15 minutes, fry out the tomato paste, then lentils and tomatoes in for 25 to 30 minutes. Two pans going, neither needing much attention.
  4. Make the rajma masala in a third pan while the bolognese simmers, then boil the pasta last so it is the freshest thing going into the fridge. The oats are assembled cold at the end and take five minutes.
  5. Total active time: about 1h45 against the 2.5 hour ceiling.

Frozen Berry and Chia Overnight Oats

6 servings15 min active32g protein11g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 3 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 half-scoops (about 90g) — from the bulk tub, not on the grocery list
  • Chia seeds, 6 tbsp (60g)
  • Frozen berry medley, 3 cups (450g)
  • Water, 2 cups (480ml)
  • Ground cinnamon, 1 tsp

Method

  1. Whisk the protein powder into the 480ml of water in a jug until it is completely smooth, before it goes anywhere near the yogurt. Powder stirred straight into Greek yogurt clumps and never recovers.
  2. In a large bowl, combine the oats, chia seeds, cinnamon and Greek yogurt, then pour in the protein water and stir until there are no dry pockets. It will look too loose; the chia takes it the rest of the way overnight.
  3. Divide between six jars, about 260g each, and press 75g of frozen berries into the top of each one. Do not stir them in — they bleed far less if they sit on top and thaw down through the oats.
  4. Lid them and refrigerate. They are ready at eight hours and eaten cold, straight from the jar.

32g protein and about 11g fibre a serving, from 50g oats, 120g Greek yogurt, a half scoop of protein powder and 10g of chia. Holds 6 days. The chia keeps thickening, so days 5 and 6 are spoonable rather than pourable — a splash of water loosens them if that bothers you. No day-of step. This is the first time this recipe has been scheduled: it is the only entry in the library never cooked, and it has been passed over six weeks running on the cost of the chia. If the $10.78 tub is not worth it to you, swapping in Cinnamon Roll Overnight Oats drops the chia and the berries and adds $1.98 of brown sugar, taking the week's recipe till from $51.49 to $38.97, which would be the cheapest week on record. It was taken here because the tub is about fifteen chia weeks, so the recurring cost of this breakfast is nearer 72 cents.

Rajma with Jeera Rice

6 servings45 min active, plus an overnight soak28g protein28g fibreIndian

Ingredients

  • Kidney beans, dried, 3 cups (570g)
  • Brown rice, 2 cups (360g)
  • Crushed tomatoes, 2 cans / 30 oz (840g)
  • Yellow onions, 2 large (450g), finely chopped
  • Fresh ginger, 2 tbsp grated (30g)
  • Garlic, 6 cloves, crushed
  • Jalapeños, 2, sliced with the seeds in
  • Cilantro, 1 bunch, chopped
  • Vegetable oil, 4 tbsp (60ml)
  • Cumin, 3 tsp — owned
  • Ground turmeric, 1.5 tsp — owned
  • Chili powder, 2 tsp — owned
  • Cayenne, 1.5 tsp — owned
  • Ground cinnamon, 1/2 tsp and black pepper, 1 tsp — the garam masala stand-in, owned
  • Salt

Method

  1. The night before, cover the kidney beans with at least three inches of cold water and leave them on the counter.
  2. Drain the beans, cover with fresh water by two inches, bring to a hard boil for 10 minutes, then simmer 45 to 55 minutes until a bean crushes easily against the side of the pan. Keep the cooking liquor — it is the body of the gravy.
  3. While the beans go, put the rice in the rice cooker with 2.5 cups of water, 2 tsp of the cumin and a pinch of salt, and leave it alone. That is the jeera rice and it needs no further attention.
  4. Heat the oil in a wide pan and fry the onions hard for 12 to 15 minutes until they are genuinely brown at the edges rather than merely soft — this is where the gravy gets its depth. Add the ginger, garlic and jalapeños and cook 2 minutes, then the turmeric, chili powder, cayenne, cinnamon and black pepper and cook 30 seconds more, until they smell toasted.
  5. Add the crushed tomatoes and cook down 10 to 12 minutes, until the oil separates out at the edges. Tip in the beans with about 2 cups of their liquor, mash a cupful of beans against the side of the pan to thicken, and simmer 15 minutes. Salt to taste.
  6. Fold the chopped cilantro through off the heat, then portion the rajma and the rice into six containers.

28g protein and about 28g fibre a serving, nearly all of it from 95g of dry kidney beans. Holds 6 days; microwave 2.5 minutes with a stir halfway and a splash of water. The cilantro is folded in on prep day rather than scattered at serving, so it mellows over the week — that is the trade for having no day-of step. The heat climbs: jalapeño seeds and cayenne keep working in the fridge, so day 6 is hotter than day 1. Brown rice stands in for basmati, which is settled policy here and worth about 2g more fibre a serving.

Lentil Bolognese with Whole Wheat Pasta

6 servings45 min active30g protein31g fibreItalian

Ingredients

  • Brown lentils, dried, 2.5 cups (450g)
  • Whole wheat pasta, 1 box / 16 oz (450g)
  • Crushed tomatoes, 2 cans / 30 oz (840g)
  • Tomato paste, 1 can / 6 oz (170g)
  • Carrots, 2 lb (900g), diced small
  • Celery, 3 stalks (250g), diced small
  • Yellow onion, 1 large (225g), diced small
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, crushed
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Dried rosemary, 1.5 tsp — owned
  • Dried oregano, 2 tsp — owned
  • Red pepper flakes, 2 tsp — owned
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Sweat the onion, carrot and celery in the oil over medium heat for 15 minutes, stirring now and then. The soffritto should collapse and sweeten; it should not brown.
  2. Push the vegetables to one side, add the tomato paste to the bare metal and fry it 2 to 3 minutes until it darkens from red to brick. Stir it back through with the garlic, rosemary, oregano and pepper flakes.
  3. Add the lentils, the crushed tomatoes and 4 cups (1 litre) of water. Bring to a simmer and cook 25 to 30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are soft and a spoon drawn through the sauce leaves a track. Salt generously — a lentil ragù takes more than you expect.
  4. Boil the pasta in well-salted water to one minute short of the packet time and drain it. Toss it with about a third of the sauce so every strand is coated rather than swimming, portion it into six containers, and spoon the remaining sauce over the top.

30g protein and about 31g fibre a serving, from 75g of dry lentils and 75g of whole wheat pasta. Holds 6 days; microwave 3 minutes covered, with a splash of water and a stir halfway. Whole wheat pasta reheats better than white because it starts firmer, and tossing it in sauce on prep day is what stops it drying out at the edges. No cheese this week — a spoonful of the dinner Greek yogurt on top after reheating does the same job for nothing. The pepper flakes climb over the six days.
