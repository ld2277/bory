Meal prep

Week of September 28

Late September in the Northeast is the week apples and winter squash are both at their absolute cheapest and best, so the week is built around them: grated apple bircher oats for breakfast, a thick curried red lentil and carrot soup for lunch, and a butternut and chickpea tagine over bulgur for dinner. The three share nothing structural — rolled oats against no grain at all against bulgur, Greek yogurt against red lentil against chickpea, Swiss against Anglo-Indian against Moroccan. The honest tension is that lunch and dinner are both warm, spiced, onion-based pot dishes; what separates them is that the soup is lean, sour and ginger-hot while the tagine is sweet with raisins and squash and carries its heat from cayenne, and breakfast is a cold jar that touches neither.

Prep order
  1. The night before: put 600g of dried chickpeas in a large bowl with plenty of cold water to soak. That is the only overnight step — the red lentils need no soak.
  2. Prep day, first thing: drain the chickpeas, cover with fresh water and set them boiling. They need 50 to 60 minutes and they are entirely passive, so everything else happens around them.
  3. While the chickpeas boil, build the six oat jars — grate the apples with the lemon juice, stir everything together, lid and fridge. About 15 minutes and then breakfast is done for the week.
  4. Start the soup next: onions, carrots, ginger, garlic and jalapenos into oil, spices in, lentils and water on top, then 25 minutes of simmering you do not need to watch.
  5. Peel and cube the squash while the soup simmers — this is the slowest knife work of the day. Build the tagine, add the drained chickpeas, and let it go 20 minutes.
  6. Bulgur last: pour boiling water over it, lid on, 15 minutes, fluff. Portion everything into six containers each.
  7. Total active time: about 1h45.

Apple and Cinnamon Bircher Oats

6 servings15 min active29g protein7g fibreSwiss

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 1 1/2 cups plus 2 tbsp (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 scoops (90g) - from the bulk tub, not on the grocery list
  • Water, 2 cups (480ml)
  • Gala apples, 3 medium / 1 lb (450g)
  • Lemon, 1, juiced
  • Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp
  • Salt, a pinch

Method

  1. Set out six jars or containers with lids.
  2. In a large bowl, whisk the protein powder into the water first, on its own, until there are no lumps. Adding it to the yogurt directly makes it seize.
  3. Stir in the Greek yogurt, the cinnamon and the pinch of salt until smooth.
  4. Grate the apples coarsely, skin on, straight into the lemon juice and toss so they do not brown. Skin on is deliberate - it is where most of the fibre is.
  5. Fold the oats and the grated apple through the yogurt mixture until everything is evenly wet.
  6. Divide between the six containers, lid them and refrigerate. They are ready to eat after about four hours and better the next morning.

29g protein and 7g fibre per serving. Holds the full 6 days in the fridge and is eaten cold straight from the jar - no morning step at all. The lemon juice is what keeps the grated apple from browning, so do not skip it. The oats thicken over the week; loosen a stiff jar with a splash of water. If you want more fibre at breakfast, a spoonful of the tagine's raisins stirred in costs nothing extra.

Curried Red Lentil and Carrot Soup

6 servings35 min active27g protein16g fibreAnglo-Indian

Ingredients

  • Red lentils, dried, 3 1/4 cups (660g)
  • Carrots, 2 lb (900g), diced small
  • Yellow onions, 2 large, diced
  • Fresh ginger, 2 tbsp grated (30g)
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, minced
  • Jalapenos, 2, sliced with the seeds in
  • Ground turmeric, 2 tsp
  • Ground cumin, 1 tbsp
  • Chili powder, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Water, 8 cups (2L)
  • Lemon, 1, juiced
  • Salt, about 2 tsp

Method

  1. Rinse the red lentils in a sieve until the water runs clear, then leave them to drain. They need no soaking.
  2. Heat the oil in your largest pot over medium heat and cook the onions with a good pinch of salt for about 8 minutes, until soft and just starting to colour.
  3. Add the carrots, ginger, garlic and jalapenos and cook another 4 minutes, stirring.
  4. Add the turmeric, cumin, chili powder and cayenne and stir them in the hot oil for a full minute - this is what stops the spices tasting raw in the finished soup.
  5. Tip in the drained lentils and the water, bring to a boil, then turn it down to a bare simmer and leave it for 25 minutes with the lid ajar.
  6. The red lentils will collapse on their own into a thick, rough puree, so there is no blending step - which is just as well, because hot soup and a very small blender is a bad combination.
  7. Stir in the lemon juice off the heat and taste for salt. It should be thick enough that a spoon stands up in it, closer to a dal than a broth.
  8. Cool for half an hour, then divide between six containers and refrigerate.

27g protein and 16g fibre per serving. Holds 6 days and reheats in the microwave in about 3 minutes, stirred halfway, with a splash of water to loosen it - it sets firm in the fridge. No flatbread this week: the standing bagels cover the carb, and dropping it is what keeps dinner's bulgur from being a second wheat in the same week. The lentils are portioned at 110g dry per serving rather than a nominal 75g, which is what carries lunch from 22g protein to 27g at no extra cost since two bags were needed either way. The heat climbs over the week as the jalapeno seeds sit in it.

Butternut and Chickpea Tagine with Bulgur

6 servings45 min active26g protein28g fibreMoroccan

Ingredients

  • Chickpeas, dried, 3 cups (600g), soaked overnight
  • Butternut squash, 1 large / about 3 lb (1.4kg) whole, giving about 2 lb (900g) prepared
  • Yellow onions, 2 large, sliced
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, minced
  • Raisins, 3/4 cup (120g)
  • Tomato paste, 1 whole can / 6 oz (170g)
  • Ground cumin, 1 tbsp
  • Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp
  • Smoked paprika, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 1/2 tsp
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Water, 3 cups (700ml)
  • Bulgur wheat, 1 1/2 cups (300g)
  • Cilantro, 1 bunch, chopped
  • Salt, about 2 tsp

Method

  1. Drain the soaked chickpeas, cover them with fresh water in a large pot and boil for 50 to 60 minutes until tender but not falling apart, then drain and set aside. Start this before anything else - it is the long pole of the whole prep day.
  2. Peel the squash, scoop the seeds and cut it into 1 inch (2.5cm) cubes. A large squash gives roughly 2 lb of flesh from 3 lb whole.
  3. Heat the oil in a wide pot over medium heat and cook the sliced onions for 10 minutes until soft and browning at the edges.
  4. Add the garlic, cumin, cinnamon, smoked paprika and cayenne and stir for a minute until it smells strong.
  5. Add the whole can of tomato paste and fry it in the oil for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring constantly, until it darkens from red to brick. This step plus the cayenne is standing in for harissa, and it is where the depth comes from.
  6. Add the squash cubes, the drained chickpeas, the raisins, the water and a good pinch of salt. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook 20 minutes until the squash is tender but still holding its shape.
  7. Uncover and simmer 5 more minutes to thicken. Taste for salt - the raisins make it sweet, so it needs more than you expect.
  8. For the bulgur: put it in a heatproof bowl, pour over 2 1/4 cups (540ml) of boiling water and 1/2 tsp salt, cover tightly and leave 15 minutes, then fluff with a fork.
  9. Portion the bulgur into the bottom of six containers, spoon the tagine over, and scatter the cilantro on top. Cool before lidding.

26g protein and 28g fibre per serving. Holds 6 days and reheats in the microwave in 3 minutes covered, stirred halfway - the bulgur underneath steams the squash back to life, so it comes out better than most reheated squash does. Two deviations worth knowing: harissa is an $11 to $15 gourmet-aisle import at Walmart, so the heat comes from cayenne, garlic and a whole fried-out can of tomato paste instead, which is the substitution the price file recommends. And couscous is unexpectedly expensive there too - no store brand exists and the cheapest single box found was $5.29 for 10 oz - so this is served over bulgur at $2.42, which does the same job under a tagine. Raisins replace dried apricots for the same reason. It is written hot at 1 1/2 tsp cayenne and the heat will climb by day 6; the Greek yogurt you already eat with dinner is the brake.
