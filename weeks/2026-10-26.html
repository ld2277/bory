Meal prep

Week of October 26

Carrot cake oats, a charred red pepper and lentil salad with feta, and a white bean and kale ribollita - the first week the ribollita has actually been schedulable, after three weeks running as the best never-cooked autumn dinner on the board and blocked every time. Kale is at its Northeast autumn peak, storage carrots are at their cheapest and sweetest, and end-of-season field peppers are exactly what you want to put under a broiler. The three separate cleanly: rolled oats, bulgur and bread for the grain, Greek yogurt, brown lentils and Great Northern beans for the protein - dairy, Lens culinaris and Phaseolus vulgaris, no species collision - and American, Levantine and Italian for the flavour base.

Prep order
  1. The night before, put the Great Northern beans in a large bowl with plenty of cold water. This is the only overnight step and the beans will not cook through in time without it.
  2. Beans on first. Drain them, boil hard for 10 minutes, then leave them simmering 45 to 55 minutes while everything else happens - they are the long pole and the rest of the day is built around them.
  3. While the beans simmer, grate the carrots and build the six oat jars, then start the lentils in a second pan and pour boiling water over the bulgur to stand for 20 minutes.
  4. Broiler on for the peppers as the lentils come off the heat, and start the ribollita soffritto on the stovetop at the same time, so the peppers steam in their covered bowl while the vegetables soften.
  5. Peel and tear the peppers, dress and assemble the salad, then finish the ribollita with the beans, kale and bread. Total active time: about 1h50.

Carrot Cake Overnight Oats

6 servings15 min active28g protein8g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 3 1/4 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 scoops (90g) - from the bulk tub, not on the grocery list
  • Water, 2 cups (480ml)
  • Carrots, 2 medium, coarsely grated / 5 oz (150g)
  • Raisins, 3/4 cup (120g)
  • Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp
  • Ground nutmeg, 1/4 tsp (optional, owned)
  • Salt, a pinch

Method

  1. Whisk the protein powder into the water in a jug until not a single lump remains. Powder stirred straight into yogurt clumps irreversibly and will not come back, which is why this is step one every oats week.
  2. Grate the carrots on the coarse side of a box grater - you want shreds that keep some bite over six days, not pulp that turns to water.
  3. Stir the Greek yogurt into the protein water until smooth and pourable.
  4. Fold in the oats, grated carrot, raisins, cinnamon, nutmeg and a pinch of salt until evenly combined, with no dry pockets of oats at the bottom.
  5. Divide between six jars or tubs, about 190g each, and seal them.
  6. Refrigerate at least eight hours before the first one is eaten, so the oats soften properly and the raisins plump.

28g protein and about 8g fibre a serving. Holds the full six days in the fridge and is eaten cold straight from the jar - grated carrot does not brown the way apple does, so there is no day-of step at all. It thickens as the week goes on; a splash of water stirred through loosens it.

Lentil, Bulgur and Charred Red Pepper Salad with Feta

6 servings45 min active28g protein17g fibreLevantine

Ingredients

  • Brown lentils, dried, 2 1/4 cups (450g)
  • Bulgur wheat, 1 3/4 cups (300g)
  • Red bell peppers, 3
  • Roma tomatoes, 8 / about 1.75 lb (800g), diced
  • Flat-leaf parsley, 1 bunch, chopped
  • Feta, crumbled, 6 oz (170g)
  • Lemons, 2 - all the juice, the zest of one
  • Vegetable oil, 1/3 cup (80ml)
  • Garlic, 3 cloves, grated
  • Ground cumin, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 1/2 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 2 tsp
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Rinse the lentils and simmer them in plenty of unsalted water for 20 to 25 minutes, until tender but still holding their shape. Drain, spread on a tray to cool fast, and salt them while they are still warm so they take it up.
  2. Put the bulgur in a bowl, pour over 2 1/4 cups (540ml) of boiling water, cover and leave it 20 minutes, then fork it through to separate the grains.
  3. Set the broiler to high and char the whole peppers on a foil-lined tray for 15 to 20 minutes, turning them until the skins are blackened and blistered all over.
  4. Seal the hot peppers in a covered bowl for 10 minutes, then slip off the skins, discard the seeds and tear the flesh into wide strips, keeping every drop of juice that collects in the bowl.
  5. Whisk the lemon juice and zest, oil, garlic, cumin, cayenne, pepper flakes and the reserved pepper juices into a dressing, and season it harder than seems sensible - both bulgur and lentils flatten salt as they sit.
  6. Fold the lentils, bulgur, peppers, diced tomatoes and all the dressing together.
  7. Stir in the parsley and the crumbled feta last, with a light hand, so the feta stays in recognisable pieces rather than smearing through.
  8. Divide between six tubs of about 400ml.

28g protein and about 17g fibre a serving. Holds six days cold - the dressing stops the grains drying out and the feta firms rather than dissolving. Eaten cold straight from the tub, with no day-of step and no reheating. The heat climbs through the week as the cayenne settles into the oil, so day five is noticeably hotter than day one; that is the intended direction, not a fault.

White Bean and Kale Ribollita

6 servings50 min active28g protein19g fibreItalian

Ingredients

  • Great Northern beans, dried, 2 1/4 cups (450g), soaked overnight
  • Kale, 2 bunches / about 1.5 lb (680g), stems stripped, leaves shredded
  • Whole wheat bread, about 8 thick slices / 10 oz (280g), a day stale, cubed
  • Crushed tomatoes, 2 cans / 30 oz (850g)
  • Celery, 4 ribs, diced
  • Carrots, 2 medium / 7 oz (200g), diced
  • Yellow onion, 1 large, diced
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, sliced
  • Jalapenos, 2, sliced with the seeds in
  • Parmesan, grated, 5 oz (150g)
  • Vegetable oil, 1/4 cup (60ml)
  • Dried rosemary, 2 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 2 tsp
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Drain the soaked beans, cover them with fresh water by two inches, boil hard for 10 minutes and then simmer 45 to 55 minutes until completely soft. Keep about 3 cups (700ml) of the cooking liquor and do not salt them until they are tender, or the skins stay tough.
  2. While the beans cook, soften the onion, carrot and celery in the oil in a large pot over medium heat for 12 to 15 minutes, until sweet and collapsing rather than browned.
  3. Add the garlic, jalapenos, rosemary and pepper flakes and fry one minute more, until the garlic smells cooked rather than raw.
  4. Stir in the crushed tomatoes and cook 10 minutes, until the sauce darkens and loses its tinny edge.
  5. Mash roughly a third of the beans with a fork, then add all the beans and the reserved liquor to the pot and bring it to a simmer. The mashed third is what gives ribollita its body without a blender.
  6. Fold in the kale a handful at a time and simmer 15 minutes, until it is silky and no longer squeaks against the spoon.
  7. Stir in the cubed bread and cook a final 10 minutes, breaking it down with the back of the spoon until the stew is thick enough to hold a spoon upright.
  8. Season hard, stir half the parmesan through, then divide between six tubs of about 450ml and keep the rest of the parmesan to scatter over each bowl after reheating.

28g protein and about 19g fibre a serving, and about 19g more protein from the 180g of Greek yogurt alongside. Holds six days and genuinely improves for the first three - ribollita means reboiled. Microwave 3 minutes covered, stirring at the halfway mark and loosening with a splash of water first, because it sets almost solid in the fridge. The reserved parmesan and the yogurt both go on after reheating, not before. The jalapeno heat climbs over the week, so no hot sauce on the side is needed.
