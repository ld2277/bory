Meal prep

Week of September 21

Three recipes that have never been in the rotation before: peanut butter and cocoa overnight oats, a black-eyed pea and collard salad with cornbread, and misir wot — Ethiopian red lentils — with braised cabbage and rice. Late September in the Northeast is when collards, cabbage and carrots are at their best and cheapest, and all three of those do real work here. Nothing overlaps: rolled oats against cornmeal against rice, dairy against black-eyed peas against red lentils, and a cold sweet jar against a vinegary Southern salad against a berbere-hot stew.

Prep order
  1. The night before: put the black-eyed peas in a bowl with plenty of cold water to soak. That is the only overnight step and it takes two minutes.
  2. Rice cooker on first with the jasmine rice — 3 cups dry and 4.5 cups water. It cooks itself while everything else happens and it is the longest passive block of the day.
  3. Drain the soaked peas and get them simmering in fresh water, 40 to 45 minutes. While they go, wash and shred the collards and slice the red onion, tomatoes and jalapenos for the salad.
  4. Start the misir wot: the four onions want 15 minutes of real browning before anything else goes in, so get them going on a second burner as soon as the peas are on. Add the berbere blend, tomato paste, lentils and water, then the cabbage and carrots, and let it simmer 30 minutes.
  5. Oven to 400F (200C) and bake the two boxes of cornbread while the wot simmers — 20 minutes, and it comes out while the lentils are still going.
  6. Assemble the oats last, in six jars, and dress the drained peas with the collards, lemon and oil. Total active time: about 1h40.

Peanut Butter and Cocoa Overnight Oats

6 servings15 min active31g protein8g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 3 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 scoops (about 90g) — from the bulk tub, not on the grocery list
  • Creamy peanut butter, 6 tbsp (95g)
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder, 6 tbsp (30g)
  • Water, 2 cups (480ml), warm
  • Salt, 1/2 tsp

Method

  1. Whisk the cocoa powder, protein powder and salt together dry in a large bowl before any liquid goes near them — cocoa clumps permanently once it is wet, and no amount of stirring afterwards fixes it.
  2. Pour in the warm water a little at a time, whisking to a smooth paste and then to a loose chocolate liquid.
  3. Add the peanut butter and whisk again until it disappears into the mixture. Warm water is what makes this easy; cold water leaves peanut butter in lumps.
  4. Fold in the Greek yogurt, then the rolled oats, until everything is evenly brown and there are no dry pockets of oats at the bottom.
  5. Divide between six jars or containers, seal, and refrigerate at least eight hours before the first one is eaten.

31g protein and 8g fibre per serving. Holds the full 6 days in the fridge and is eaten cold straight from the jar — no reheating, no morning assembly. The oats keep absorbing liquid over the week, so days 5 and 6 will be noticeably thicker; a splash of water stirred in loosens them back. The peanut butter is doing flavour work rather than protein work here — the yogurt and powder carry that.

Black-Eyed Pea and Collard Salad with Cornbread

6 servings40 min active28g protein16g fibreSouthern US

Ingredients

  • Black-eyed peas, dried, 1 lb 5 oz (600g), soaked overnight
  • Collard greens, 2 bunches / about 1.5 lb (700g)
  • Red onion, 1 medium, sliced thin
  • Roma tomatoes, 4, diced
  • Jalapenos, 2, sliced thin with the seeds left in
  • Garlic, 3 cloves, minced
  • Lemons, 2, juiced
  • Vegetable oil, 1/3 cup (80ml)
  • Smoked paprika, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 1 tsp
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Corn muffin mix, 2 boxes (480g)
  • Eggs, 2 large
  • Water, 2/3 cup (160ml), for the cornbread

Method

  1. Drain the soaked black-eyed peas, cover them with fresh cold water in a large pot, and simmer 40 to 45 minutes until tender but still holding their shape. Salt them in the last ten minutes. Drain and spread them on a tray to cool — peas dressed while hot go mushy.
  2. Heat the oven to 400F (200C). Stir the two boxes of corn muffin mix with the eggs and water until just combined, pour into a greased 8-inch square pan, and bake 20 minutes until the top springs back. Cool completely before cutting into six squares.
  3. Strip the collard leaves off their stems, stack them, roll them, and slice into thin ribbons. Wash well in a bowl of cold water — collards hold grit — and spin or towel them dry.
  4. Put the ribbons in a large bowl with a teaspoon of salt and the lemon juice and massage them with your hands for two full minutes. The leaves will darken and soften and lose about a third of their volume. This is what makes raw collards pleasant to eat.
  5. Warm the oil gently with the garlic, smoked paprika, cayenne and red pepper flakes for about a minute, until it smells alive but the garlic has not browned. Pour it straight over the collards while hot.
  6. Add the cooled peas, red onion, tomatoes and jalapenos, season with black pepper and more salt, and toss. Divide between six containers, with the cornbread squares stored separately.

28g protein and 16g fibre per serving. Holds 6 days and is eaten cold — massaged collards do not wilt the way spinach or lettuce would, and the salad is better on day 3 than on day 1. Store the cornbread in its own container or bag, not on top of the salad, or it goes soggy by day 2; it is fine at room temperature all week. The heat climbs over the week as the jalapeno seeds sit in the oil, which is intended. The second bag of black-eyed peas leaves about 300g spare.

Misir Wot with Cabbage and Rice

6 servings45 min active27g protein16g fibreEthiopian

Ingredients

  • Red lentils, dried, 1 lb 3 oz (540g)
  • Yellow onions, 4 large / about 2 lb (900g), diced fine
  • Green cabbage, half a head / about 1.5 lb (700g), shredded
  • Carrots, 4 medium / 1 lb (450g), sliced into coins
  • Garlic, 6 cloves, minced
  • Tomato paste, 1 can, 6 oz (170g)
  • Vegetable oil, 1/2 cup (120ml)
  • Berbere blend: smoked paprika 3 tbsp, cayenne 1.5 tbsp, chili powder 1 tbsp, ground cumin 2 tsp, cinnamon 1 tsp, red pepper flakes 1 tsp
  • Salt, 2 tsp
  • Water, 6 cups (1.4L)
  • Jasmine rice, 3 cups dry (600g)
  • Water for the rice, 4.5 cups (1.1L)

Method

  1. Put the jasmine rice and its water in the rice cooker and start it. It will be done long before the wot and holds fine.
  2. Cook the diced onions in a large dry pot over medium heat for about ten minutes, stirring, with no oil at all — this is how misir wot is actually built, and the onions release their own water and collapse into a sweet base.
  3. Add the oil and the garlic and cook another five minutes, until the onions are genuinely browned at the edges rather than merely soft. This is the single step that decides whether the dish tastes deep or flat, so do not rush it.
  4. Stir in the berbere blend and cook it in the oil for a full minute until it darkens and smells toasted, then add the tomato paste and cook another two minutes.
  5. Add the red lentils, the salt and 6 cups of water. Bring to a boil, then drop to a low simmer and cook 20 minutes, stirring now and then so nothing catches on the bottom.
  6. Add the shredded cabbage and carrot coins, stir them through, and simmer another 15 minutes until the lentils have broken down into a thick stew and the carrots are tender. It should be thick enough that a spoon leaves a track. Loosen with water if it goes past that.
  7. Divide the rice between six containers and ladle the wot over the top. Cool uncovered for 20 minutes before the lids go on.

27g protein and 16g fibre per serving. Holds 6 days. Reheat in the microwave for 3 minutes covered, stirring at the halfway point — the lentils thicken hard in the fridge, so add a tablespoon of water before reheating and stir again at the end. This is the hottest dish in the rotation so far and the capsaicin will keep climbing through the week; if day 6 is too much, a spoon of the Greek yogurt that comes with dinner cuts it cleanly. Proper berbere also carries fenugreek, ginger and cardamom — this blend is an approximation built from spices already in the cupboard rather than a $6-plus specialty jar.
