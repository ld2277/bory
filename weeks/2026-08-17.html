Meal prep

Week of August 17

Peak Northeast summer, so the week is built on the three things at their best right now: zucchini and spinach in a frittata, sweet corn charred with poblanos in a cold bean salad, and a red curry that leans on green beans. The three stay clear of each other on every axis — eggs, then pinto beans, then tofu; a bagel, then the corn itself, then brown rice; Italian, then Mexican, then Thai. Protein lands at 146g a day against a 140g target, but fibre from the three recipes is about 24g — the frittata is a low-fibre breakfast by nature, and it takes the standing wholewheat bagel and banana to clear 30g.

Prep order
  1. The night before, cover the pinto beans with cold water and leave them to soak — everything else on this list happens on the day.
  2. Start by putting the brown rice in the rice cooker and the drained beans on the stove to simmer, because both then run unattended for the next hour while you work.
  3. Heat the broiler and char the corn and poblanos first, then seal the poblanos in a covered bowl to steam, which loosens the skins with no attention from you.
  4. Drop the oven to 375F, cook the frittata vegetables on the stovetop and get the frittata in — its 35 minutes of oven time is your window to cut the corn off the cobs, peel and dice the poblanos and whisk the salad dressing.
  5. Fry the tofu and build the curry last so it is the only thing still hot when the rest has cooled, and assemble the corn salad while the curry simmers.
  6. Total active time: about 1h45.

Zucchini, Spinach and Cottage Cheese Frittata

6 servings25 min active, 35 min in the oven28g protein2g fibreItalian

Ingredients

  • Eggs, large, 18
  • Cottage cheese, 2 cups / 16 oz (450g)
  • Zucchini, 2 large / 2 lb (900g)
  • Baby spinach, 5 oz (140g)
  • Scallions, 1 bunch, whites and greens kept separate
  • Garlic, 3 cloves, minced
  • Vegetable oil, 2 tbsp (30ml)
  • Red pepper flakes, 2 tsp
  • Salt, 2 tsp total, and black pepper, 1 tsp

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 375F (190C) and oil a 9x13 inch (23x33cm) baking dish.
  2. Coarsely grate the zucchini, toss it with 1 tsp of the salt and leave it in a colander for 10 minutes, then squeeze it dry in a clean towel — a wet frittata will not hold six days.
  3. Warm the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat and cook the squeezed zucchini with the scallion whites and garlic for 6 to 8 minutes, until the pan is dry and the zucchini is lightly browned.
  4. Add the spinach a handful at a time and stir until it has fully wilted and any water has cooked off, then take the pan off the heat and let it cool for 5 minutes.
  5. Beat the eggs with the cottage cheese, red pepper flakes, remaining salt and the black pepper until only small curds remain.
  6. Stir the cooled vegetables and the sliced scallion greens through the eggs, pour everything into the baking dish and bake for 30 to 35 minutes, until the centre is set and no longer wobbles when you nudge the dish.
  7. Cool the frittata completely on a rack before cutting it into six squares, because cutting it warm makes it weep, then refrigerate in covered containers.

28g protein and 2g fibre per serving. Keeps 6 days covered in the fridge. Eat it cold, or microwave a square 45 to 60 seconds — any longer and the eggs go rubbery. Eaten with a wholewheat bagel from the staples, which adds about 4g of fibre.

Charred Corn, Poblano and Pinto Salad with Queso Fresco

6 servings30 min active, plus an overnight soak and an unattended simmer28g protein14g fibreMexican

Ingredients

  • Dried pinto beans, 2 1/4 cups / 1 lb (450g), soaked overnight
  • Sweet corn, 6 ears
  • Poblano peppers, 3
  • Jalapenos, 2, sliced paper thin
  • Red onion, 1 large, diced small
  • Queso fresco, 10 oz (280g), crumbled
  • Limes, 2, juiced, plus the third for the curry
  • Cilantro, 1 bunch, chopped
  • Garlic, 2 cloves, minced, plus 1 whole clove for the beans
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Ground cumin, 2 tsp; smoked paprika, 2 tsp; cayenne, 1 tsp; salt

Method

  1. Drain the soaked beans, cover them with fresh water by two inches, add the whole garlic clove and simmer for 60 to 75 minutes until tender but not collapsing, salting only in the last 10 minutes.
  2. While the beans cook, char the corn and the poblanos under a hot broiler or directly over a gas flame, turning until they are blackened in patches all over, about 8 to 10 minutes.
  3. Seal the poblanos in a covered bowl for 10 minutes, then rub off the blackened skins, pull out the seeds and dice the flesh.
  4. Stand each cob on end and cut the kernels off in slabs, scraping the cob afterwards for the milky bit at the base.
  5. Whisk the oil, lime juice, cumin, smoked paprika, cayenne, minced garlic and a generous pinch of salt into a dressing that tastes slightly too sharp on its own.
  6. Drain and cool the beans, then toss them with the corn, poblano, jalapeno and red onion while everything is still a little warm, so the dressing soaks in.
  7. Fold through half the queso fresco and most of the cilantro, then divide into six containers and refrigerate.

28g protein and 14g fibre per serving. Keeps 6 days and is eaten cold, no reheating. Hold back the remaining queso fresco and cilantro and scatter them on at serving, so they stay fresh rather than going soft in the dressing. The jalapeno heat climbs noticeably by day four.

Thai Red Curry with Tofu

6 servings35 min active, rice cooker does the rest31g protein7g fibreThai

Ingredients

  • Extra-firm tofu, 3 blocks / 3 lb (1.36kg) total, pressed and cubed
  • Brown rice, 1 1/2 cups dry / 11 oz (300g)
  • Coconut milk, 2 cans / 27 fl oz (800ml) total
  • Thai red curry paste, 4 tbsp (60g)
  • Green beans, 12 oz (340g), topped and cut in half
  • Poblano pepper, 1, sliced
  • Garlic, 3 cloves, minced
  • Vegetable oil, 2 tbsp (30ml)
  • Lime, 1, zest and juice
  • Sugar, 2 tsp, and salt

Method

  1. Put the brown rice in the rice cooker with its usual water and start it before anything else, so it is done and cooling by the time the curry is finished.
  2. Press the tofu between two plates under a weight for 15 minutes, then cut it into 3/4 inch (2cm) cubes and pat them dry.
  3. Fry the tofu in the oil over medium-high heat in a wide pan, in two batches so it browns rather than steams, about 8 minutes a batch, then lift it out onto a plate.
  4. Turn the heat down, add the curry paste and garlic to the same pan and fry for 2 minutes until it darkens and smells toasted — this step is what separates a good curry from a flat one.
  5. Pour in the coconut milk, bring it to a gentle simmer and cook for 5 minutes, then stir in the sugar and taste for salt.
  6. Add the green beans and poblano and simmer for 6 to 8 minutes, until the beans are tender with a little bite left, since they will soften further over the week.
  7. Return the tofu to the pan, simmer 3 minutes more, then take it off the heat and stir through the lime zest and juice.
  8. Cool the curry and the rice separately, then portion into six containers with the rice underneath and the curry on top.

31g protein and 7g fibre per serving. Keeps 6 days. Reheat in the microwave for 3 minutes covered, stirring once at the halfway mark so the coconut milk does not split at the edges. Serve with the 180g of Greek yogurt on the side rather than stirred in. Four tablespoons of curry paste is a properly hot curry that gets hotter by day five, so keep a wedge of lime back if you want to pull it down.
