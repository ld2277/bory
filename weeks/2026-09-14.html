Week of September 14

Two dried-legume mains and a cold oat jar, built on the cheapest shape a week can take. Mid-September is the last good stretch for field tomatoes and sweet corn in the Northeast and the first genuinely good one for sweet potatoes, so the gigantes gets ripe Romas and the chili gets both. Nothing overlaps: rolled oats against pita against quinoa, Greek yogurt against large lima beans against pinto beans, and American against Greek against Tex-Mex.

Prep order

Carrot Cake Overnight Oats

6 servings15 min active29g protein7g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 3 1/3 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 half-scoops (90g) — from the bulk tub, not on the grocery list
  • Water, 2 cups (480ml)
  • Carrots, coarsely grated, 2 1/2 cups (270g)
  • Raisins, 3/4 cup (120g)
  • Light brown sugar, 1/4 cup (48g)
  • Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp
  • Salt, 1/2 tsp

Method

  1. Grate the carrots on the coarse side of a box grater; all 270g takes about five minutes and needs no food processor.
  2. In a large bowl, whisk the Greek yogurt, protein powder, water, brown sugar, cinnamon and salt until no lumps of powder remain.
  3. Stir in the oats, grated carrot and raisins and mix until every oat is coated.
  4. Divide between six jars, press the mixture level, leave a little headroom, seal and refrigerate.
  5. Give the first jar at least eight hours before eating; the raisins plump and the carrot softens overnight.

About 29g protein and 7g fibre per serving. Holds the full six days sealed in the fridge and is eaten cold straight from the jar — no reheating and no morning assembly. If a jar has tightened up by day five, loosen it with a splash of water. The protein powder comes out of the bulk tub, so it never appears on the grocery list.

Gigantes Plaki with Feta and Pita

6 servings35 min active, plus an overnight soak27g protein22g fibreGreek

Ingredients

  • Large lima beans, dried, 3 cups (570g)
  • Roma tomatoes, 10 / about 2.2 lb (1kg), chopped
  • Crushed tomatoes, 1 can / 15 oz (425g)
  • Yellow onions, 2 large / about 1 lb (450g), diced
  • Garlic, 6 cloves, sliced
  • Fresh dill, 1 bunch, chopped
  • Crumbled feta, 6 oz (170g)
  • Vegetable oil, 1/3 cup (80ml)
  • Dried oregano, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 1/2 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 1 tsp
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Whole wheat pita, 6, stored separately

Method

  1. The night before, cover the lima beans with plenty of cold water and leave them to soak.
  2. Drain the beans, cover with fresh water in a large pot, bring to a boil and simmer 45 to 60 minutes until tender but still holding their shape, then drain and keep a cup of the cooking liquid.
  3. While the beans cook, heat the oven to 400F (200C) and soften the onion and garlic in the oil in a wide skillet over medium heat for about 8 minutes.
  4. Add the chopped Romas, the crushed tomatoes, the oregano, cayenne and red pepper flakes and simmer 10 minutes until the sauce thickens and darkens.
  5. Fold the drained beans into the sauce with the reserved cooking liquid, season generously, and transfer to a baking dish.
  6. Bake uncovered 40 to 45 minutes, until the top has blistered and the edges have caught.
  7. Cool, stir through half the dill, then divide between six containers and scatter the feta and the remaining dill over each one.

About 27g protein and 22g fibre per serving, nearly all of it from the lima beans. Holds six days and is good cold or microwaved for 2 minutes. Keep the pita in its bag on the counter and warm one for 20 seconds when you eat — stored with the beans it turns to paste. The cayenne climbs over the week, which suits. If the bean portion lands heavy, use three-quarters of them and accept 25g protein at lunch.

Pinto and Sweet Potato Chili with Quinoa

6 servings35 min active, plus an overnight soak27g protein20g fibreTex-Mex

Ingredients

  • Pinto beans, dried, 2 1/4 cups (454g)
  • Quinoa, white, 2 1/4 cups (390g)
  • Sweet potatoes, 2 medium / 1.75 lb (800g), peeled and cubed
  • Sweet corn, 3 ears, kernels cut off
  • Chipotles in adobo, 1 can / 7 oz (198g), chopped, adobo and all
  • Crushed tomatoes, 1 can / 15 oz (425g)
  • Yellow onion, 1 large, diced
  • Green bell pepper, 1, diced
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, minced
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Ground cumin, 1 tbsp
  • Chili powder, 1 tbsp
  • Smoked paprika, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp
  • Dried oregano, 1 tsp
  • Salt

Method

  1. The night before, soak the pinto beans in cold water in a separate pot from the lima beans.
  2. On prep day, drain the pintos, cover with fresh water and simmer 60 to 75 minutes until tender, then drain and keep two cups of the cooking liquid.
  3. Put the quinoa and 4 1/2 cups (1.1L) of water in the rice cooker on the white rice setting and leave it alone; it is done in about 25 minutes and asks nothing of you.
  4. Heat the oven to 425F (220C) and roast the cubed sweet potato in one tablespoon of the oil for 25 minutes, turning once.
  5. In a large pot, soften the onion, green pepper and garlic in the remaining oil for 8 minutes, then add the cumin, chili powder, smoked paprika, cayenne and oregano and fry 1 minute until fragrant.
  6. Add the chopped chipotles, the crushed tomatoes, the drained beans and the reserved bean liquid, and simmer 20 minutes.
  7. Fold in the roasted sweet potato and the corn kernels in the last five minutes so they keep their shape, then season hard.
  8. Divide the quinoa between six containers and spoon the chili over it.

About 27g protein and 20g fibre per serving. Holds six days. Reheat in the microwave for 3 minutes covered, stirring at the halfway point so the quinoa underneath heats through. The whole can of chipotles makes this properly hot and it climbs by day four; a spoonful of the Greek yogurt on the side takes the edge off, and that is where the 19g of banked yogurt protein comes from.
