Meal prep

Week of October 5

A Greek bean bake, a Japanese curry and a jar of banana oats — three dishes that share nothing but a fridge shelf. The week leans on what storage carrots and onions do best in early October and keeps the tomatoes canned rather than chasing late fresh ones. Breakfast is dairy on oats, lunch is lima beans with a feta accent and no grain at all, and dinner is soy protein on brown rice, so the carb, the protein and the cuisine all land in different places.

Banana and Honey Overnight Oats

6 servings10 min active29g protein8g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 3 1/3 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g) — one 32 oz tub
  • Protein powder, 3 scoops (90g) — from the bulk tub, not a grocery line
  • Honey, 6 tbsp (120g)
  • Ground cinnamon, 1 1/2 tsp
  • Water, about 2 cups (480ml)
  • Salt, 1/2 tsp
  • Bananas, 6 — sliced in at serving, from the standing bunch

Method

  1. Whisk the protein powder into the water in a large bowl until no lumps remain, then stir in the honey, cinnamon and salt.
  2. Add the Greek yogurt and whisk until the mixture is smooth and pourable.
  3. Stir in the rolled oats and fold until every oat is coated.
  4. Divide between six jars, seal them, and refrigerate overnight; the oats will be fully softened by morning.
  5. Each morning, slice a whole banana into the jar just before eating, and loosen with a splash of water if it has set too thick.

29g protein and 8g fibre per serving. Holds the full 6 days in the fridge and is eaten cold straight from the jar, so there is no reheating at all. The banana is the week's only day-of step and takes thirty seconds — slicing it in on prep day would brown it by day two. The bananas come out of the standing weekly bunch, so they are not a separate grocery line, and the protein powder comes from the bulk tub.

Gigantes Plaki with Feta

6 servings30 min active, plus an overnight soak and about 1h30 of passive cooking and baking27g protein23g fibreGreek

Ingredients

  • Large dried lima beans, 1 lb 7 oz (660g) — one 32 oz bag, about 250g left over
  • Crushed tomatoes, two 15 oz cans (two 425g cans)
  • Tomato paste, 1 can, 6 oz (170g)
  • Yellow onions, 2 large / 1 lb (450g), diced
  • Garlic, 5 cloves, sliced
  • Vegetable oil, 1/3 cup (80ml)
  • Crumbled feta, 6 oz (170g)
  • Fresh dill, 1 bunch, leaves chopped
  • Lemon, 1
  • Dried oregano, 2 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. The night before, cover the lima beans with plenty of cold water and leave them to soak; they will roughly double in size.
  2. Drain the soaked beans, cover them with fresh water in a large pot, bring to a boil and simmer 40 to 50 minutes until tender but not collapsing, then drain and keep 2 cups (480ml) of the cooking liquid.
  3. While the beans cook, heat the oven to 400F (200C) and soften the diced onion and sliced garlic in the oil in a wide ovenproof pan over medium heat for about 10 minutes.
  4. Add the tomato paste and fry it out for 3 to 4 minutes until it darkens from red to brick, then stir in the crushed tomatoes, oregano, red pepper flakes and cayenne.
  5. Fold in the drained beans with 1 1/2 cups (360ml) of the reserved cooking liquid, season generously, and bring the pan to a simmer.
  6. Bake uncovered for 40 to 45 minutes, until the surface is blistered and the sauce has thickened around the beans.
  7. Let the pan cool, then divide between six containers and top each with the feta and the chopped dill.
  8. Squeeze the lemon over the portions just before sealing them.

27g protein and 23g fibre per serving, the heaviest fibre slot of the week. Holds 6 days and is good either cold or microwaved 2 minutes — these beans do not go mealy. The feta and dill go in on prep day rather than at serving, because the dish eats better once they have melted into the sauce. There is no pita this week: the standing bagels cover the carb, and dropping it saved $2.58 and kept the week's three grains genuinely distinct. The heat will climb over the six days as the pepper flakes sit in the oil.

Tofu Katsu Curry with Brown Rice

6 servings45 min active28g protein10g fibreJapanese

Ingredients

  • Extra-firm tofu, two 16 oz blocks (two 450g blocks)
  • Panko bread crumbs, 1 1/2 cups (120g)
  • All-purpose flour, 1/2 cup plus 2 tbsp (70g)
  • Brown rice, 2 1/4 cups (450g) dry
  • Carrots, 2 lb (900g), sliced into coins
  • Yellow onion, 1 large / 8 oz (225g), diced
  • Frozen sweet peas, 1 1/2 cups (200g)
  • Curry powder, 3 tbsp
  • Soy sauce, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Vegetable oil, 1/3 cup (80ml)
  • Jalapenos, 2, sliced with the seeds in
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp
  • Turmeric, 1 tsp
  • Salt

Method

  1. Start the brown rice in the rice cooker with 4 1/2 cups (1.08L) of water, so it cooks while everything else happens.
  2. Press the tofu blocks between two plates under a weight for 15 minutes, then cut each block into three slabs, which gives six cutlets.
  3. Heat the oven to 425F (220C) and line a baking sheet.
  4. Dust each cutlet in a little of the flour, brush it with water, press it firmly into the panko on both sides, and set it on the sheet.
  5. Drizzle the breaded cutlets with half the oil and bake them 25 minutes, turning once, until the crust is golden and crisp.
  6. Meanwhile, soften the diced onion and sliced carrots in the remaining oil in a large pot over medium heat for 10 minutes.
  7. Stir in the rest of the flour and cook it for 2 minutes, then add the curry powder, turmeric, cayenne and sliced jalapenos and fry for 1 minute more.
  8. Pour in 4 cups (950ml) of water along with the soy sauce, whisking to keep the sauce smooth, and simmer 15 minutes until it coats a spoon.
  9. Add the frozen peas, simmer 2 minutes, and season with salt.
  10. Divide the rice and sauce between six containers, and store the six cutlets separately in one sealed container.

28g protein and 10g fibre per serving. Everything holds 6 days. The rice and sauce microwave in 3 minutes with a stir halfway. The cutlet is the week's one reheat compromise: 5 minutes in a hot oven brings the crust back, and in the microwave it goes soft but eats perfectly well sliced into the curry. Storing the cutlets apart from the sauce is what keeps them from turning to paste. The jalapeno seeds will make the sauce noticeably hotter by day 4 or 5, and the Greek yogurt already eaten with dinner is the brake if day 6 is too much.
