Meal prep

Week of September 7

Apple and cinnamon bircher oats, a charred corn, poblano and pinto salad, and a chickpea shakshuka you finish with fresh eggs each evening. Early September in the Northeast is the one week that gives you both ends of the calendar at once — corn, poblanos and Roma tomatoes at their peak and the first Gala apples on the shelf — so all three recipes are built on produce that is cheap and good right now. Nothing overlaps: oats, no grain, and bread on the side; Greek yogurt, pinto beans, and eggs with chickpeas; Swiss, Mexican and Levantine.

Prep order

  4. Assemble the corn salad once the pintos are drained and cooled, fold the chickpeas into the finished sauce, and portion everything out. Total active time: about 1h45.

Apple and Cinnamon Bircher Oats

6 servings10 min active28g protein7g fibreSwiss

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 3 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 half-scoops (90g) — from the bulk tub, not on the grocery list
  • Water or milk, 2 cups (480ml)
  • Gala apples, 3 medium / 1 lb (450g)
  • Lemon, 1
  • Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp
  • Salt, a pinch

Method

  1. Combine the oats, Greek yogurt, protein powder, cinnamon and a pinch of salt in a large bowl and stir until the powder is fully worked in with no dry pockets left.
  2. Add the water a little at a time, stirring, until the mixture falls slowly off a spoon — it thickens overnight, so err on the wet side.
  3. Grate two of the apples coarsely with the skin on, straight into the bowl, and squeeze over the juice of half the lemon, tossing immediately so the apple does not brown.
  4. Divide between six jars, press the surface flat, and refrigerate overnight before eating the first one.
  5. Keep the third apple whole and grate a little fresh over the top on days five and six, with a squeeze of the remaining lemon, if you want the texture back.

28g protein and about 7g fibre per serving. Holds six days in the fridge and is eaten cold straight from the jar, so there is no morning step at all. The lemon juice on the grated apple is what keeps it from browning across the week; the reserved third apple is optional insurance for the back end.

Charred Corn, Poblano and Pinto Salad with Queso Fresco

6 servings40 min active28g protein17g fibreMexican

Ingredients

  • Pinto beans, dried, 2 3/4 cups (540g)
  • Sweet corn, 6 ears
  • Poblano peppers, 3
  • Queso fresco, 10 oz (283g)
  • Red onion, 1 medium
  • Limes, 2
  • Cilantro, 1/2 bunch
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Ground cumin, 2 tsp
  • Chili powder, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp or more
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Soak the pinto beans overnight in plenty of cold water, or bring them to a boil for two minutes and leave them off the heat for an hour.
  2. Drain the beans, cover them with fresh water by two inches, and simmer for 50 to 70 minutes until tender but not collapsing, salting only in the last ten minutes.
  3. While the beans cook, rub the corn and the poblanos with a little of the oil and char them under the oven broiler or directly over a gas flame, turning until blackened in patches all over.
  4. Let the poblanos steam in a covered bowl for ten minutes, then rub off the loose skin, discard the seeds and chop them; cut the kernels off the corn cobs with a knife.
  5. Dice the red onion finely and toss it with the juice of both limes and a heavy pinch of salt, leaving it five minutes to soften its bite.
  6. Combine the drained beans, corn, poblano and lime-soaked onion with the cumin, chili powder, cayenne and the remaining oil, then taste for salt and heat.
  7. Fold through half the chopped cilantro, crumble the queso fresco over the top, and divide between six containers.

28g protein and about 17g fibre per serving — the pintos are doing almost all of that, at 90g dry per serving. Holds six days in the fridge and is eaten cold, no reheating. Keep the rest of the cilantro whole in the fridge and scatter it fresh over each portion, since chopped herbs go dark after two days. The cayenne climbs over the week, so start at a teaspoon.

Shakshuka with Chickpeas and Crusty Bread

6 servings45 min active26g protein16g fibreLevantine

Ingredients

  • Chickpeas, dried, 2 cups (360g)
  • Eggs, 12 large
  • Roma tomatoes, 10 / about 2 1/4 lb (1kg)
  • Poblano pepper, 1 (the spare from the lunch bag)
  • Yellow onions, 2 medium
  • Garlic, 1 head
  • Tomato paste, 6 oz (170g)
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Ground cumin, 1 tbsp
  • Smoked paprika, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 1 tsp
  • Whole wheat bread, 1 loaf
  • Cilantro, 1/2 bunch
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Soak the chickpeas overnight, then simmer them in fresh water for about 90 minutes until tender, or cook them in the rice cooker on its longest setting to keep the stovetop free for the beans.
  2. Dice the onions and the poblano and soften them in the oil in a wide pan over medium heat for about ten minutes, until translucent and just starting to colour.
  3. Add the whole head of garlic, peeled and sliced, along with the cumin, smoked paprika, cayenne and pepper flakes, and fry for one minute until the spices smell toasted.
  4. Stir in the tomato paste and cook it for two minutes so it darkens, then add the chopped Roma tomatoes and a splash of water.
  5. Simmer the sauce uncovered for 25 to 30 minutes, until it is thick enough to hold a channel when you drag a spoon across the pan.
  6. Fold in the drained chickpeas, season hard with salt and black pepper, and simmer for five minutes more.
  7. Cool the sauce completely and divide it between six containers — do not cook the eggs now, they will not survive storage.
  8. Each evening, tip one portion into a small pan or a wide bowl and reheat it, then crack two eggs into wells in the sauce and cover until the whites set: about five minutes on the stove, or three to four minutes in the microwave at 60 percent power with a plate on top.
  9. Scatter with cilantro and eat with a thick slice of toasted whole wheat bread.

26g protein and about 16g fibre per serving, counting the two eggs and the slice of bread. The sauce base holds six days and reheats in the microwave in three minutes with a stir halfway. The eggs are the one day-of step in the week — poached eggs do not hold, so they go in fresh each evening and add about five minutes. Heat builds in the sauce as the week goes on; keep hot sauce on the side if you want the back half milder.
