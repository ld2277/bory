Peanut Butter and Cocoa Overnight Oats

6 servings12 min active32g protein7g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, old fashioned, 3 1/3 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 scoops (about 90g) — from the tub you already own, not on the grocery list
  • Peanut butter, creamy, 6 tbsp (90g)
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder, 6 tbsp (36g)
  • Water or milk, 2 cups (480ml)
  • Ground cinnamon, 1 tsp
  • Salt, 1/2 tsp

Method

  1. Whisk the protein powder into the 480ml of water in a jug first, until no lumps remain. This is step one every oats week for a reason: powder stirred straight into yogurt clumps irreversibly and will not come apart.
  2. Whisk the cocoa powder and the salt into that same liquid while it is still thin, so the cocoa hydrates instead of sitting on top as dust.
  3. Fold the Greek yogurt into the cocoa liquid in a large bowl, then stir in the oats and the cinnamon until everything is evenly dark.
  4. Divide between six jars, about 240g each, and drop a level tablespoon of peanut butter into each. Stir it through or leave it as a seam — either holds.
  5. Lid them and refrigerate. They are ready the next morning and good through day six.

32g protein and about 7g fibre per serving. Eaten cold straight from the jar with no day-of step. Both the cocoa and the peanut butter deepen over the week rather than fading. Longest-rested recipe in the library — last cooked the week of Sep 21.

Black-Eyed Pea and Collard Salad with Cornbread

6 servings45 min active27g protein13g fibreSouthern US

Ingredients

  • Black-eyed peas, dried, 1 lb (450g)
  • Collard greens, 2 bunches, about 1.5 lb (680g)
  • Roma tomatoes, 6
  • Red onion, 1 medium
  • Lemon, 1
  • Garlic, 3 cloves
  • Vegetable oil, 6 tbsp (90ml)
  • Red pepper flakes, 2 tsp
  • Smoked paprika, 2 tsp
  • Cayenne, 1 tsp
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Corn muffin mix, Jiffy, 2 boxes (482g)
  • Eggs, 2
  • Plain Greek yogurt thinned with water, 2/3 cup (160ml), standing in for milk

Method

  1. Rinse the black-eyed peas and cover them with cold water by three inches. They need no soak. Bring to a boil, then simmer 45 to 55 minutes until tender but not collapsing, salting only in the last ten minutes. Drain and spread them out to cool.
  2. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Stir the two boxes of muffin mix together with the two eggs and the thinned yogurt, pour into a greased 8-inch square pan and bake 25 to 28 minutes until a skewer comes out clean. Cool completely before cutting into six squares.
  3. Strip the collard leaves off their ribs, stack them and shred into thin ribbons. Massage them with 2 tbsp of the oil, the juice of half the lemon and a good pinch of salt until they darken and soften — two full minutes of real hand work, and it is what lets them hold six days.
  4. Whisk the remaining oil with the rest of the lemon, the grated garlic, red pepper flakes, smoked paprika and cayenne.
  5. Dice the tomatoes and slice the red onion thin. Toss everything with the cooled peas and the dressing, taste hard for salt and acid, and divide into six containers.
  6. Store the cornbread separately in a bag or box so it does not go soggy against the dressing.

27g protein and about 13g fibre per serving. Eaten cold, no reheating. Massaged collards are the only green that survives six days dressed; a tender leaf would be slime by Wednesday. Cornbread stored apart — good at room temperature, or 15 seconds in the microwave.

Red Lentil and Coconut Dal with Sweet Potato

6 servings35 min active27g protein15g fibreSri Lankan

Ingredients

  • Red lentils, dried, 1 lb (450g)
  • Brown rice, 2 cups (360g) dry
  • Coconut milk, 2 cans (800ml)
  • Sweet potatoes, 2 lb (900g)
  • Baby spinach, 8–10 oz (225–280g)
  • Yellow onions, 2 medium
  • Garlic, 5 cloves
  • Fresh ginger, 2 tbsp grated (30g)
  • Jalapeños, 2, seeds in
  • Cilantro, 1 bunch
  • Vegetable oil, 3 tbsp
  • Curry powder, 2 tbsp · turmeric, 2 tsp · cumin, 2 tsp · cayenne, 1 1/2 tsp · ground cinnamon, 1/2 tsp
  • Salt

Method

  1. Put the brown rice in the rice cooker with 4 1/2 cups (1.07L) of water and start it. Everything below fits inside its 40 to 45 minutes.
  2. Rinse the red lentils until the water runs clear — three or four changes.
  3. Heat the oil in a large pot and sweat the diced onion with a good pinch of salt for 8 minutes, until soft and just turning gold. Add the grated ginger, the garlic and the jalapeños sliced with their seeds in, and cook 2 minutes more.
  4. Add the curry powder, turmeric, cumin, cayenne and cinnamon and fry them in the oil for 45 seconds, until they smell toasted rather than raw. This is the step that separates a good dal from a dusty one.
  5. Add the lentils, the sweet potato peeled and cut into 3/4-inch (2cm) cubes, both cans of coconut milk and 3 cups (700ml) of water. Bring to a boil, then simmer 22 to 25 minutes, stirring now and then, until the lentils have collapsed and the sweet potato is tender to a fork.
  6. Stir the spinach through in handfuls off the heat until it wilts, then the chopped cilantro. Salt hard — a coconut dal takes more than you expect. Portion over the rice into six containers.

27g protein and about 15g fibre per serving — the sweet potato, coconut and spinach carry what bulgur or pasta would, which is why 75g of dry lentil clears the target here even over rice. Microwave 3 minutes covered with a splash of water, stirring halfway; it thickens as it sits and by day four the splash is not optional. Cilantro is stirred in on prep day rather than at serving, so there is no day-of step anywhere this week — it mellows, and that is the accepted trade. Heat climbs over the week from the jalapeño seeds and the cayenne. First cook of this recipe.
