Meal prep

Week of November 16

Banana and honey oats, a Greek gigantes bake and a Cajun jambalaya — the jambalaya is a first cook, and it has been quietly affordable for months without anyone scheduling it. Mid-November is the right moment for both mains: the gigantes is a winter tomato braise arriving in its season, and the jambalaya runs on storage onions, celery and peppers with canned tomato doing the rest. The three share nothing — rolled oats against no grain against brown rice, Greek yogurt against large lima beans against kidney beans, American against Greek against Cajun — though the two mains are both beans, in different species, and that is the closest this week comes to a collision. Meals supply 84g of protein against the 81g the rules ask for; with the 40g shake and about 19g of Greek yogurt at dinner the day lands at 143g. The most expensive line is honey at $4.50, which is also the week's one expensive-aisle item; nothing was cut to make budget, and the margin against the $55 ceiling is $8.28, the roomiest in over a month.

Prep order
  1. The night before: soak the large lima beans and the kidney beans in separate bowls of cold water. Both need it, and neither will cook through in time without it.
  2. Rice cooker on first with the brown rice — 450g of rice to about 1.1 litres of water, roughly 45 minutes, entirely passive and it can sit warm until the jambalaya is ready for it.
  3. Drain and boil both pots of beans at once: limas 45 to 55 minutes, kidneys 45 to 55 minutes. While they go, build the gigantes sauce and chop the trinity for the jambalaya.
  4. Limas into the baking dish with the sauce and the oven at 400F for 40 to 50 minutes. The kidney beans and the jambalaya finish on the stovetop in that window, and the oats get made last — they take five minutes and need no heat.
  5. Total active time: about 1h45, inside the 2.5 hour ceiling.

Banana and Honey Overnight Oats

6 servings10 min active30g protein8g fibreAmerican

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 1 1/2 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 scoops (90g) - from the tub you already own
  • Water, 2 cups (480ml), plus a little more to loosen
  • Honey, 6 tbsp (120g)
  • Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp
  • Fine salt, 1/2 tsp
  • Bananas, 6 - sliced in at serving, not now

Method

  1. Whisk the protein powder into the water in a jug until there are no lumps at all. Do this before the powder goes anywhere near the yogurt - stirred straight into yogurt it clumps and the clumps never come out.
  2. Stir the yogurt into the jug, then the honey, cinnamon and salt, until it is a smooth pourable cream.
  3. Divide the oats between six jars, about 50g each, and pour the yogurt mixture over. Stir each one so no dry oats sit at the bottom.
  4. Lid them and refrigerate overnight. They will be ready by morning and will keep thickening over the week - loosen with a splash of water on day five or six if they have gone stiff.

30g protein and 8g fibre a serving. Keeps 6 days in the fridge, eaten cold, no reheating. One day-of step: slice half a banana into the jar at serving rather than on prep day, or it browns and goes slimy by day two. Honey is the week's one expensive-aisle item at $4.50 - swapping to brown sugar at $1.98 would make this the cheapest breakfast on the board, at the cost of the flavour this recipe is named for.

Gigantes Plaki with Feta

6 servings35 min active, 50 min in the oven26g protein19g fibreGreek

Ingredients

  • Large lima beans, dried, 1 1/2 cups (660g) - soaked overnight
  • Yellow onions, 2 large / about 1 lb (450g), diced
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, sliced
  • Crushed tomatoes, 2 cans / 30 oz (850g)
  • Tomato paste, 1 can / 6 oz (170g)
  • Vegetable oil, 1/3 cup (80ml)
  • Fresh dill, 1 clamshell / about 3/4 oz (20g), chopped, stalks and all
  • Crumbled feta, 5 oz (142g)
  • Cayenne, 1 1/2 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 2 tsp
  • Dried oregano, 2 tsp
  • Lemon, 1, juiced
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Drain the soaked limas, cover them with fresh water in a large pot, bring to a boil and simmer 45 to 55 minutes until they are tender enough to crush against the side of the pot but still whole. Drain, keeping a cup of the cooking water.
  2. While they cook, heat the oil in a wide pan and sweat the onions over medium heat for 10 minutes until soft and sweet. Add the garlic, cayenne, red pepper flakes and oregano and cook one minute more.
  3. Add the whole can of tomato paste and fry it, stirring, until it darkens from red to brick - about three minutes. This is where the depth comes from, so do not rush it.
  4. Pour in the crushed tomatoes and the reserved bean water, season well, and simmer 10 minutes until it thickens.
  5. Fold in the drained beans and half the dill, tip everything into a baking dish, and bake at 400F for 40 to 50 minutes until the top is caramelised and the sauce has pulled away from the edges.
  6. Scatter the feta over and return it to the oven for the last 10 minutes. Off the heat, stir through the lemon juice and the rest of the dill.

26g protein and 19g fibre a serving. Keeps 6 days, microwave 2 minutes - it is also excellent cold, which is how a gigantes is usually eaten in Greece. No day-of step. The pita the library entry calls for is dropped: the standing bagels do that job and it saves $2.58. The heat is cayenne and pepper flakes rather than fresh chili, and it will climb noticeably by day four.

Vegetarian Jambalaya with Red Beans

6 servings40 min active28g protein23g fibreCajun

Ingredients

  • Kidney beans, dried, 3 cups (570g) - soaked overnight
  • Brown rice, 2 1/4 cups (450g) dry
  • Yellow onions, 2 large / about 1 lb (450g), diced
  • Celery, 4 stalks / about 0.6 lb (280g), diced
  • Green bell peppers, 2 / about 0.9 lb (400g), diced
  • Jalapenos, 2, seeds in, minced
  • Garlic, 4 cloves, minced
  • Diced tomatoes, 2 cans / 29 oz (820g)
  • Vegetable oil, 1/4 cup (60ml)
  • Smoked paprika, 1 tbsp
  • Cayenne, 2 tsp
  • Dried oregano, 2 tsp
  • Red pepper flakes, 1 tsp
  • Salt and plenty of black pepper

Method

  1. Put the brown rice in the rice cooker with about 1.1 litres of water and start it. It takes roughly 45 minutes and needs no attention; leave it on warm when it finishes.
  2. Drain the soaked kidney beans, cover with fresh water, boil hard for 10 minutes and then simmer 45 to 55 minutes until tender. The hard boil matters - undercooked kidney beans are genuinely unpleasant. Drain.
  3. Heat the oil in your largest pan and cook the onion, celery and green pepper over medium-high heat for 12 to 15 minutes, until the onion is browning at the edges rather than just soft. This browning is most of the flavour in a meatless jambalaya.
  4. Add the garlic, jalapenos, smoked paprika, cayenne, oregano and red pepper flakes and cook two minutes until it smells toasted.
  5. Add the diced tomatoes with their juice, season hard, and simmer 10 minutes until the liquid has reduced by about half.
  6. Fold in the cooked kidney beans and the cooked rice, a third at a time so the rice does not turn to paste. Simmer five minutes more, taste, and correct the salt - this dish takes more than you expect.

28g protein and 23g fibre a serving. Keeps 6 days, microwave 3 minutes covered with a splash of water and a stir halfway - the rice dries out at the edges otherwise. No day-of step. Kidney beans are at 95g dry a serving rather than the usual 75g because rice carries only about 4.5g of protein and there is nothing else in the dish to make it up. First time this one has been cooked.
