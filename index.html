Nothing overlaps: rolled oats against cornbread against brown rice, and a Swiss bircher against a Southern salad against a Japanese curry. Zero expensive-aisle items and zero new spices, which is what buys the $6.86 margin — the week's most expensive lines are two bunches of collards and two blocks of tofu, tied at $4.94, and nothing was cut to make budget.

Apple and Cinnamon Bircher Oats

6 servings15 min active28g protein8g fibreSwiss

Ingredients

  • Rolled oats, 1 1/2 cups (300g)
  • Plain Greek yogurt, 3 cups (720g)
  • Protein powder, 3 half-scoops (90g) — from the tub you already own, not on the list
  • Milk or water to loosen, about 2 cups (480ml)
  • Gala apples, 3 medium / 1 lb (450g)
  • Lemon, 1
  • Ground cinnamon, 2 tsp

Method

  1. Whisk the protein powder into the milk or water in a jug first, until there are no lumps. Powder stirred straight into yogurt clumps irreversibly and will not come back.
  2. Stir the oats, the Greek yogurt and the protein liquid together in a large bowl with the cinnamon, until the mixture is loose enough to fall off a spoon. It thickens overnight, so err slightly wet.
  3. Divide between six jars and refrigerate.
  4. Grate one apple coarsely with the skin on and toss it with the juice of half a lemon so it does not brown. Stir it through the first two jars.
  5. On the remaining days grate half a fresh apple into the jar at serving with a squeeze of lemon. That is the one day-of step, and it takes under a minute.

About 28g protein and 8g fibre a serving. Holds 6 days, eaten cold. The apple is the only part that does not keep — grate it fresh from day three onward. No sweetener: storage apples in late November are sweet enough with cinnamon, and leaving the brown sugar out keeps $1.98 off the shop.

Black-Eyed Pea and Collard Salad with Cornbread

6 servings35 min active29g protein16g fibreSouthern US

Ingredients

  • Black-eyed peas, dried, 1 1/4 lb (570g)
  • Collard greens, 2 bunches / 1.5 lb (680g)
  • Roma tomatoes, 6 / 1.3 lb (600g)
  • Red onion, 1, sliced thin
  • Yellow onion, 1, quartered, for the bean pot
  • Garlic, 4 cloves
  • Jalapeno, 1, seeds in
  • Lemon, 1
  • Vegetable oil, 1/4 cup (60ml)
  • Cayenne 1 tsp, smoked paprika 2 tsp, red pepper flakes 1 tsp — all owned
  • Corn muffin mix, Jiffy, 2 boxes, made with water only

Method

  1. Rinse the black-eyed peas and put them in a pot with the quartered yellow onion, two crushed garlic cloves and plenty of water. They need no soak. Simmer 45 to 55 minutes until tender but not collapsing, then drain and spread them on a tray to cool.
  2. While they cook, strip the collard leaves off their stems, stack and roll them, and shred them as finely as you can. Massage the shredded leaves with a tablespoon of oil and a good pinch of salt for a minute — that is what makes raw collard pleasant on day six instead of leathery.
  3. Make the dressing: the juice of one lemon, 3 tbsp oil, two grated garlic cloves, the jalapeno finely chopped with its seeds, 1 tsp cayenne, 2 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp red pepper flakes, salt and plenty of black pepper.
  4. Bake the cornbread: mix both boxes of Jiffy with water only, following the box, and bake it in one pan. Water rather than milk keeps the dish off the dairy line and the texture is no worse.
  5. Toss the cooled peas, the shredded collards, the diced tomatoes and the sliced red onion through the dressing. Divide between six tubs, cut the cornbread into six and store it in a separate box.

About 29g protein and 16g fibre a serving — the peas carry roughly 23g at 95g dry and the two boxes of cornbread add about 6g. Holds 6 days, eaten cold, and massaged shredded collard genuinely improves by day two. Keep the cornbread in its own box or it goes soggy. The heat climbs noticeably across the week.

Tofu Katsu Curry with Rice

6 servings45 min active27g protein8g fibreJapanese

Ingredients

  • Extra-firm tofu, 2 blocks / 2 lb (907g)
  • Brown rice, 1 1/2 cups dry (300g)
  • Panko bread crumbs, about 1 cup (120g)
  • All-purpose flour, 3/4 cup (90g)
  • Carrots, 1 lb (450g), diced
  • Yellow onion, 1 large, diced
  • Garlic, 4 cloves
  • Jalapeno, 1, seeds in
  • Frozen sweet peas, 1 1/2 cups (200g)
  • Soy sauce, 3 tbsp (45ml)
  • Curry powder 3 tbsp, turmeric 1 tsp, cayenne 1 1/2 tsp — all owned
  • Vegetable oil, about 1/2 cup (120ml) for frying plus 2 tbsp for the sauce

Method

  1. Start the brown rice in the rice cooker — 300g dry with its usual water. It takes about 45 minutes and needs nothing from you.
  2. Press the tofu: stand both blocks on a plate with something heavy on top for 20 minutes while you make the sauce. Water left in the block is what makes a soggy cutlet.
  3. For the sauce, heat 2 tbsp oil in a pan and soften the diced onion, carrot, garlic and the chopped jalapeno with its seeds for 8 minutes. Stir in 3 tbsp of the flour and cook it for a minute, then the curry powder, turmeric and cayenne for 30 seconds until it smells toasty. Pour in 3 cups (700ml) water and the soy sauce, whisking, and simmer 15 minutes until it coats a spoon. Add the frozen peas in the last 2 minutes.
  4. Cut each tofu block into three slabs, giving six cutlets. Dredge each one in seasoned flour, then a thin flour-and-water slurry, then press it hard into the panko on both sides.
  5. Shallow-fry the cutlets in about 1cm of oil, 3 minutes a side, until deep gold. Drain them on a rack rather than on paper so the undersides stay crisp.
  6. Portion rice and sauce into six tubs and lay one cutlet on top of the rice rather than in the sauce.

About 27g protein and 8g fibre a serving. Holds 6 days. Reheat: microwave the rice and sauce 3 minutes covered with a stir halfway, then either crisp the cutlet 5 minutes in a hot oven or eat it soft — both are fine by day five. Store the cutlet on the rice and never submerged in the sauce, or it turns to paste by day three. The cayenne and the jalapeno both climb over the week; a katsu curry this hot is unorthodox and deliberate.
